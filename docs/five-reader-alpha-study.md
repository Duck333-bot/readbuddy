# ReadBuddy Five-Reader Alpha Study

## Purpose

This is not a design-preference survey. The purpose is to identify where a real reader loses clarity, trust, or momentum between first visit and the first moment ReadBuddy proves it understands the book.

Each participant should bring a genuinely difficult book they are currently reading. Do not demonstrate the product or explain every feature. Say only:

> Upload something you are currently reading and use ReadBuddy however you naturally would.

## Session Setup

Each session should take 30–40 minutes. Use a screen recording only with the participant’s consent. Record the time of each observed funnel event in the owner dashboard, but do not copy book passages, selected text, or personal questions into the research notes.

| Moment | What to observe | Pass signal |
|---|---|---|
| Landing | Does the reader understand the promise without help? | They can explain that ReadBuddy understands the book, remembers progress, and avoids spoilers. |
| Upload | Can they choose a PDF and understand readiness? | They find upload unassisted and notice that reading can begin before deeper analysis ends. |
| Reading | Can they find the basic reading controls? | They turn pages or continue reading without asking for navigation help. |
| Highlight | Do they discover the natural AI action? | They highlight a confusing sentence and select Explain without prompting. |
| Answer | Does the response feel attached to the book? | They describe it as grounded in the reading, not a generic chatbot. |
| Evidence | Do citations make sense? | They open an earlier passage and understand the return path. |
| Return | Can they leave and resume later? | They know how to return to Library and locate the current journey. |

## Required Follow-up Questions

Ask these exactly after the natural session, without asking whether they “like the UI.”

1. What felt confusing or annoying?
2. Was there a moment ReadBuddy understood something you did not expect it to?
3. Was there any AI answer you did not trust?
4. If you were going to read another 30 pages tonight, would you use ReadBuddy or go back to your normal reading app? Why?
5. If ReadBuddy disappeared tomorrow, what would you actually miss?

## Decision Rule After Five Sessions

Stop coding after the fifth session. Cluster the observation notes by repeated problem, then rank them by frequency and severity. The next sprint should fix only the top three repeated issues. Do not add the Book Brain completion pulse unless at least two readers are confused about whether the book understanding is ready.
